A subject's signed delegation can let an app act for them. A `TokenSession` carries the `delegationChain` a delegated token was issued on; `Stack.open(adapter, { verifyDelegation })` takes the verifier that validates one, and `asActor(session)` takes such a session as it takes any other, validating its chain on every operation, with the stack's own revocations, and throwing the new `DelegationRefusedError` once it no longer validates. A valid delegation counts on the principal's side of the permission check beside grants naming the principal, evaluated as a grant would be (write implies read); a principal acting for someone else never reaches a family that governs authority. `Stack.verifyDelegationChain(chain, principalId)` runs the check alone, for issuing a token. `verifyAuthChallenge()` takes a `verifyDid` callback that, when given, decides every DID. A `DidCredential` may carry a `delegationChain`, `parseAuthTokenBody(body, { delegations: true })` accepts one (and refuses it otherwise), and `StackTokenStore.createToken()` takes a session in the shape `lookupToken()` returns. New `_revocation@1` system type: a Record naming a delegation's CID revokes it when written by the owner or by an issuer upstream in its chain, recoverable like a grant; `Stack.isDelegationRevoked(cid, issuers)` answers whether one does. New exported types: `VerifiedDelegation`, `DelegationVerifier`, `RecordVerb`, `DidSignatureVerifier` and `RevocationContent`.
