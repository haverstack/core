A subject's signed delegation can let an app act for them. A `Session` is an `Actor` with an optional `delegationChain`; `TokenSession` is one with `principalId` populated, and both `asActor(session)` and `StackTokenStore.createToken(session)` take it. `Stack.open(adapter, { verifyDelegation })` takes the verifier that validates a chain, and a delegated view validates it on every operation through `Stack.verifyDelegationChain(chain, principalId)`, with the stack's own revocations, throwing the new `DelegationRefusedError` (an authentication failure, answered with 401) for every refusal, a stack without a verifier included. A refused delegation ends a subscription. A valid delegation counts on the principal's side of the permission check beside grants naming the principal, evaluated as a grant would be (write implies read), and never reaches an ungrantable family. `verifyAuthChallenge()` takes a `verifyDid` callback that, when given, decides every DID. A `DidCredential` may carry a `delegationChain`, and `parseAuthTokenBody(body, { delegations: true })` accepts one (and refuses it otherwise). New `_revocation@1` system type, ungrantable and owner-written: `revokeDelegation(chain)` on `Stack` (the owner) and `ScopedStack` (an issuer of a link in the chain, acting as itself) writes one naming the chain's last delegation, `Stack.isDelegationRevoked(cid)` answers whether one does, and `parseRevocationBody()` reads `POST /revocations`. New exported types: `Session`, `VerifiedDelegation`, `DelegationCheck`, `DelegationVerifier`, `RecordVerb`, `DidSignatureVerifier`, `RevocationContent` and `WireRevocationRequest`.
